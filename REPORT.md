# 📑 Wildfire Impact & ESG Environmental Risk Assessment Report (1988–2030)

**Author:** Sinem Akyaman  
**Domain:** ESG Data Analytics, Environmental Risk Modeling, Climate Science  
**Data Sources:** General Directorate of Forestry (OGM) & Global Forest Watch (GFW)

---

## Executive Summary

This report presents a comprehensive data-driven evaluation of historical wildfire patterns, cause-specific drivers, and regional biodiversity risks across Türkiye from 1988 to 2024, alongside predictive ML risk projections through 2030.

By integrating multi-source government statistics with Global Forest Watch (GFW) satellite-derived tree cover loss metrics, this study bridges biological domain expertise with environmental data science to support corporate ESG strategies, ecosystem restoration policies, and proactive climate risk management.

---

## 🔍 Key Findings & Chart Interpretations

### 1. Regional Hotspots & Geographic Vulnerability (2024 Breakdown)
* **Finding:** In 2024, **Manisa, İzmir, Bolu, Aydın, and Çanakkale** emerged as the top provinces experiencing the highest acreage losses due to wildfires.
* **ESG & Climate Risk Insight:** Western Anatolian and Aegean forest ecosystems face escalating climate vulnerability. Localized early-detection networks, real-time satellite monitoring, and rapid-response infrastructure are critical for corporate and public risk mitigation in these high-value biodiversity corridors.

### 2. Anthropogenic Drivers: Negligence vs. Intentional Causes
* **Finding:** Historical data analysis (1997–2024) reveals that **human negligence** (uncontrolled picnics, agricultural stubble burning, power line maintenance issues) consistently dominates as the primary cause of wildfire escalation, accounting for more than 70% of total burned hectares in peak years.
* **ESG Policy Insight:** Unlike natural drivers (e.g., lightning strikes), human negligence represents a preventable operational risk. Public awareness campaigns, stricter agricultural enforcement, and utility infrastructure upgrades (power grid line safety) can directly mitigate the majority of annual forest loss.

### 3. Tree Cover & Ecosystem Biodiversity Loss Index
* **Finding:** Integrating biological domain metrics with Global Forest Watch tree cover loss data shows sharp ecosystem damage spikes during major disaster years—most notably the 2008, 2015, and the historic 2021 wildfire crisis.
* **ESG & Carbon Offset Insight:** Severe wildfires cause long-term habitat fragmentation and severe loss of carbon sequestration capacity. Quantifying biodiversity damage via ecosystem impact coefficients enables organizations to more accurately measure their scope 3 environmental impact and biodiversity footprint.

## 📈 Predictive Risk Forecasting (2025–2030)

Using Scikit-Learn Linear Regression models trained on historical cause-specific data (1997–2024), future burn coverage trends were projected through 2030:

* **2030 Projected Annual Negligence Loss:** **~14,416 Hectares**
* **2030 Projected Annual Intentional Loss:** **~7,350 Hectares**

### Strategic Takeaways for 2030:
* Without proactive systemic intervention, human-driven wildfire occurrences will continue a steady upward trajectory through 2030.
* Targeted policy interventions focusing on agricultural burning regulations, energy grid fireproofing, and community outreach can save thousands of hectares of forest cover and preserve critical regional flora/fauna every year.

## 🛠️ Methodology & Technical Pipeline

1. **Data Ingestion & Cleaning:** Automated Python pipeline using Pandas to handle encodings (`windows-1254`), correct multi-format decimal/thousands separators, and align multi-source data schemas.
2. **Feature Engineering:** Derived `Biodiversity_Damage_Index` metrics based on hectares burned and forest density variables.
3. **Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA):** Multi-panel visualization dashboards built with Matplotlib and Seaborn.
4. **Predictive Analytics:** Supervised machine learning models trained with Scikit-Learn to project cause-specific risk trends.
